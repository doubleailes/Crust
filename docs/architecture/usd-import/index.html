The USD import

Crust Render reads every stage through Moho, a scene index over openusd built on the model of Hydra 2.0.

Through a scene index

The renderer never walks USD prims itself. Every stage is read through Moho, a scene index over the pure-Rust openusd crate, on the model of Hydra 2.0, the scene description layer of Pixar's OpenUSD:

  • a scene index gives each prim a Hydra type (mesh, camera, sphereLight, …) and its data as data sources, read lazily;
  • filters stack on top of it, as UsdImaging stacks them. Native instancing and point instancing produce prototypes, flattening composes transforms and visibility, material binding resolution answers which material each prim renders with, and light linking turns collections into categories;
  • the index owns the time, so a render at -f 12 reads every value at frame 12, and motion blur reads transforms at the shutter's offsets around it.

Crust is a render delegate over that stack, as hdPrman or hdEmbree are over UsdImaging's. It observes the index's prims as they are added, removed and dirtied, keeps one managed prim per prim, and commits them into a scene in path order: meshes, curves, implicit shapes, lights, cameras, materials, instancers, volumes, render settings and products, each by its Hydra type. It reads the Hydra schemas only. That is why its own crust:* attributes are constant primvars on geometry and volumes, which Hydra carries, and why a missing value reads as the schema's default.

A few facts are not scene description: the layers a stage composed, an override authored to hide a prim, where a material's specs come from. The program that opened the stage, the host, answers those. For crust render that is the command line. The delegate itself never names a stage.

One stage

Crust reads one stage, payloads loaded, as a Hydra render delegate is handed one scene index. Moho composes the Moana Island whole at about 7 GiB, so there is no chunking. Light links are installed after the commit, once every light has been seen.

Updates

A host that keeps the delegate, such as Mantle's viewport, edits the stage and asks for an update. The delegate reads what the index reports as changed. Render settings and the camera, a material's inputs, a light's parameters, a light added or removed, a light's or a receiver's light links, an area light moved, and a mesh's transform or points (a mesh another prim shares, and a mesh inside an instanced prototype, included) are updated in place. Anything else is committed again from the prims it keeps, with its textures still decoded, after the scene it replaces is let go. Either way the scene is exactly the one a fresh render would read.

The lights are a layer of their own: their geometry is numbered after every other geometry and kept in a part of the acceleration structure of its own, so a light added, removed or moved rebuilds the lights alone. A scene with area lights pays a little for it on every ray (usdlux renders about 7% slower).

Prototypes

Moho gives each instanced prototype one path. A prototype is therefore built once for the whole render and placed wherever it is used. Warnings still name the prims by their USD paths, not by the index's.

One openusd

Crust reaches openusd only through Moho, which pins it to one revision, the one Mantle uses too. A bump moves Moho first, then Crust Render, then Mantle.
